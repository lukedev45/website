# What is Quantum Entanglement?

Quantum mechanics is the most sucessful theory of physics that exist today; I will not delve into the history of quantum mechanics in this blog because I believe it deserves its own entry. However, there is a fantastic comic called **[Introducing Quantum Theory: A Graphic Guide](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/25537096-introducing-quantum-theory?ac=1&from_search=true&qid=cwYprKDldX&rank=1)** which can be read in a weekend and will inform you on the development of quantum theory. In fact, the book leaves off right where will we pick up -- the EPR paper and Bell's inequality.

---

Einstein was frustrated with the state of quantum mechanics; *wave function* this and *uncertatinty principle* that.
But he was especially conflicted with the idea that he coined "spooky action at a distance".

Locality is the notion that objects can only interact with each other if they are part of each others immediate surroundings. Even if you imagine a phone call between two people, this conversation is mediated by the radio waves travelling between their cell phones. This is the speed of light, and according to the laws of physics it can not be exceeded.
Imainge a set of dominos standing up right and they are all arranged in a line, it is very clear that tipping the first domino will result in a cascade of dominos falling one after the other.
It, usually, never occurs in reality that pushing one domino will simuntaneously and immediately control another domino.
Or imagine a case with a Voodoo doll. If you were unlucky enough for someone to curse you and for them to put part of your soul into a voodoo doll then the person controlling the voodoo could inflict pain and misery into your life at a moments notice with **no delay**.
In modern day physics we call this **non-locality**.

If we want to understand quantum entanglement we will refer to the person who was trying to do the same thing, Albert Einstein.
